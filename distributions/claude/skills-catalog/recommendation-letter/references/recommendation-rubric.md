# Recommendation-Letter Rubric — Seven-Rule Checklist

Use this checklist before handing a draft to the recommender and again after rendering any
requested document.

- [ ] **1. Relationship stated up front.** The opening says who the recommender is to the
      subject and how they know them, including duration or capacity when supplied.
- [ ] **2. No fabrication.** Every claim traces to a fact the recommender actually holds.
      Missing a strengthening detail? Ask the recommender. Mark unresolved facts in an
      incomplete draft rather than inventing them.
- [ ] **3. Concrete evidence over adjectives.** Include at least one specific thing the
      subject did or the recommender observed. Connect it to the opportunity; do not rely
      on a paragraph of qualities alone.
- [ ] **4. Confidence matches knowledge.** Remove repetitive "I believe / I feel / I think"
      when they add nothing, while preserving material uncertainty and the scope of the
      recommender's experience. Do not assert guaranteed success or stronger endorsement
      than the supplied facts and judgment support.
- [ ] **5. Reframe deficits honestly.** Use a constructive interpretation only when evidence
      supports it. Do not turn an unexplained gap into invented deliberateness or intent,
      and do not let a limitation dominate the letter.
- [ ] **6. Offer follow-up.** Include an explicit offer to be contacted and the recommender's
      chosen contact details under their name. Do not supply an inferred email or phone.
- [ ] **7. Respect the requested length and presentation.** Aim for one page and three or
      four tight paragraphs unless the recipient or user requires otherwise. Cut repetition.
      For a PDF, inspect the actual pages before claiming it meets the length requirement.

**Private output:** keep filled letters and subject details outside source repositories and
public workspaces. Use the user's requested private destination and verify it; a conventional
folder name does not prove that a path is ignored by Git or access-controlled. Send only with
the user's explicit authorization, and never imply a signature or review that has not occurred.
